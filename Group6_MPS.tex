\begin{abstract}
People numbered $1$ to $n$ stand in a circle. Starting with person $1$, every
second person is eliminated until only one is left, and $J(n)$ denotes the
number of this survivor. We first played the game by hand for
$n=1,\dots,16$. The results suggested three rules: $J(2k)=2J(k)-1$,
$J(2k+1)=2J(k)+1$, and the fact that every $n$ can be written in exactly one
way as $n=2^m+\ell$ with $0\leq\ell<2^m$. We proved them with one lemma: the
game only depends on the places of the people around the circle, not on their
numbers, so after one turn of the circle it becomes a smaller copy of itself.
We then proved the formula
\[
J(2^m+\ell)=2\ell+1
\]
in two ways, by induction on $m$ and by induction on $n$.
We generalised it to a game that starts with any person $s$: the survivor is
the person $2\ell$ places after $s$. In base $2$, the formula becomes a simple
rule, since $J(n)$ is obtained by moving the first digit of $n$ to the end.
This gives at once, for example, $J(10^9)=926\,258\,177$. Finally, we explain
the Python code we wrote to simulate the game and to check every result.
\end{abstract}
